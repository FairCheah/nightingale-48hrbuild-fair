MODELS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Anthropic Claude Haiku 4.5   (claude-haiku-4-5-20251001)
  Used for three narrow jobs, each a separate call with its own prompt:
    1. Risk classification  — src/lib/nightingale-ai.ts
    2. Conversational reply — src/lib/nightingale-ai.ts
    3. Fact extraction      — src/lib/memory.ts
  Plus triage summarisation (src/app/chat/escalate.ts) and the shareable
  card (src/lib/value-events.ts).

  Chosen as the cheapest and fastest model in the range. Every job here is
  narrow and tightly constrained by a system prompt; none needed a larger
  model, and cost per conversation matters for a clinic.

  Accessed through the Anthropic Messages API. Governed by Anthropic's
  Commercial Terms of Service. The model is not fine-tuned, and no data
  from this build was used for training.

  Anthropic's commercial retention policy deletes API inputs and outputs
  within thirty days, and up to two years where a session is flagged for a
  usage-policy violation. Everything this build sends across that boundary
  has already been redacted, which is why redaction runs before the model
  rather than after it.

  src/lib/llm.ts is provider-agnostic. Nothing above that file knows which
  model answered; swapping providers is a change to one file.

ground the assistant's low-risk answers. Each names the organisation that
publishes on the topic:

  World Health Organization   who.int    fact sheets on infertility,
                                         endometriosis, PCOS, STIs, cervical
                                         cancer, contraception, maternal
                                         mental health
  NHS (UK)                    nhs.uk     conditions pages on infertility,
                                         IVF, periods, vaginal discharge,
                                         pregnancy, miscarriage, menopause
  NICE (UK)                   nice.org.uk  NG88, heavy menstrual bleeding

IMPORTANT: these entries were written for this build. They are paraphrases
restricted to broadly accepted public-health statements, not quotations, and
they were not written or reviewed by a clinician. A real deployment replaces
this file with clinician-reviewed content; no other code changes.

LANGUAGE COVERAGE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
The deterministic emergency floor covers English, Bahasa Malaysia, and the
code-switched Manglish patterns Malaysian patients commonly use. Malay
patterns were written and checked by the author, who speaks the language.

Mandarin and Tamil are NOT covered. Both are widely spoken by Malaysian
patients. They were omitted rather than guessed at: an unverified emergency
pattern is worse than an absent one, because it looks like coverage.
Closing this needs a native speaker with clinical vocabulary.


DEVELOPMENT TOOLS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Claude (Anthropic) was used as a pair programmer throughout: architecture
discussion, code generation, debugging, and drafting written deliverables.
Every design decision, every departure from the brief, and every line that
shipped was reviewed and accepted by the author. Several of the build's
better decisions — scope-aware routing, the refusal to act on likes, the
in-scope/out-of-scope split — came from questioning what was proposed.

Round Two continued that. Reading a triage screen and finding the sentence
did not make sense surfaced a redaction bug that had been deleting clinical
language; asking whether the retention purge had ever actually run surfaced
one that reported success while deleting nothing. Neither was caught by the
test suite, which passed throughout.

VS Code, Git, Supabase SQL Editor.
